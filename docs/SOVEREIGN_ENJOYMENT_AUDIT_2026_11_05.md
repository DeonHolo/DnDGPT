# Sovereign enjoyment audit and proposed corrections

5 November 2026. Review of the current 1.3.2 Sovereign Laws book, hardener, creation validator, test fixture and shared GM instructions. This records the original audit. The subsequent approved decisions are summarized below; current implementation and release evidence are in SOVEREIGN_CREATION_AND_LEVELS_2026_11_05.md.

## Missing starting Sovereign choices: confirmed implementation gap

The creation section of `dndgpt-app/rules/sovereign/laws/rulebook.txt` promises six opening core-power developments, choosing two, and six Sovereign developments, choosing two. These are additional developments of the chosen kit. The starting Domain, General and forces are not themselves expenditure of those choices.

`kit_check` in `dndgpt-app/app/sovereign_laws.py` checks the body, four Laws, identities, Domain and rarity. It does not check that either opening development bundle has been applied. `validate_selections` validates boards that are supplied; it does not require both opening boards. The shared `build` fixture in `dndgpt-app/tests/test_sovereign_laws.py` likewise supplies no opening core or Sovereign upgrades. Therefore passing tests did not establish delivery of the promised starting rewards. This is verified from the implementation, not a claim about the exact tool sequence in the player's current campaign, which was not opened.

Proposed correction: explicitly surface both starting bundles to the GM, verify the actual selected developments on their owners before calling a new build complete, and test the complete creation path with both bundles. Respect explicit custom counts and delegated selection. Existing active characters should not become blocked by a newly introduced creation check. An omitted opening award remains owed and should be presented and applied without rebuilding the character or adding a dashboard approval.

## Other findings

| Finding | Evidence and status | Proposed change |
|---|---|---|
| Compulsory weakness | The book's creation/body sections and the body validator require a real vulnerability. Verified hard requirement. | Make a special weakness optional. Derive danger from the actual opponent, environment and established body. Preserve existing facts without inventing a handicap for each new class. |
| Invented costs and preparation | The powers section permits genuine conditions and says to use costs when they affect play. It does not require a cost for every power, but leaves room for the GM to invent material or preparation taxes. Interpretation risk. | Establish useful ordinary operation first. A special cost or preparation condition should come from the chosen concept and actual effect; do not add one merely to compensate for an interesting or high-rarity power. |
| Charging an upgrade for ordinary creativity | Powers allow supported creative uses now, while upgrades must open a plan the owner could not attempt before. These need a clearer boundary. | Let ordinary applications of an owned power work now. Upgrades add a genuine new capability, medium, transformation or repeatable permission; they should not sell back a reasonable use the original power already supports. |
| Frequent complications | On even footing, the d6 gives a setback on 1–2, success with a cost on 3–4, and clean success on 5–6. Each band is one-third. Success is two-thirds, but only one-third is clean. Verified arithmetic. | Roll for meaningful uncertainty and opposition. Routine or decisively outclassed tasks can succeed outright. A mixed result still delivers the agreed objective; a cost must not erase the victory or repackage the same minor obstacle. Any change to the actual dice bands needs a separate decision. |
| Unpredictable progression | The book awards two core and two Sovereign upgrades at a meaningful chapter breakthrough, without a clear progress schedule. It has no defined ordinary Luck-growth schedule or definite Class-ascension route. Verified gaps. | Show the next growth reward and a clear attainable trigger. Define Luck growth and a one-tier Class-ascension route. Numbered levels and fourth-level bonuses remain design decisions, not changes approved by this audit. |
| Titles can drag play into society | The book asks who saw or learned about the deed; the hardener says witnesses; the validator requires recognition text. A mandatory human audience is not stated, but the wording can encourage one. | Let a deed be recognized by the System, enemies, creatures or the sovereign's own forces. Keep the earned capability and distinguish it from who currently knows the name. Avoid compulsory introductions or reputation errands. |
| Surprises are too narrowly eligible | The new book makes random surprises optional on significant journeys or new frontiers. Bound-domain play can spend many scenes outside that wording. The archived embodied hardener explicitly included meaningful defense and domain work. Verified scope difference. | Define eligible material scene transitions that include defense, hunts and domain activity, with at most one contextual check per transition. Include opportunities, discoveries and strange creatures as well as danger. Reuse the existing batched roll/save path. Causal world developments remain independent of random surprises. |
| Action-focused preference weakened | Archived `rules/sovereign/personal/section-43.txt` specifies roughly 70% personal adventure and 30% strategy in mixed mode. The current Laws book preserves focus/dialogue preferences but omits that balance. Verified omission. | Restore action-first pacing as a flexible campaign preference. For a bound being, direct embodied action can happen through its Domain or minions. Routine logistics stay in the background under authorized orders. |
| Positive writing is unevenly available | The shared GM skill explicitly omits caveat lists, hypothetical-misuse warnings and unchanged-stat assurances. The standalone Laws book/hardener do not carry that explicit contract. Verified document difference. | Put a concise positive-effect writing rule in the canonical Laws source and checklist: current effect, relevant trigger, actual changes and brief flavor. Explain boundaries when asked or when a proposed action needs adjudication. |
| Legacy instructions share the active wrapper | The shared GM skill begins with current twelve-class/six-option Laws guidance, but also contains older eight-class/five-option, Background, action-economy and stat instructions marked for archived profiles. These are scoped, not equally authoritative current rules. | Separate profile-specific operating instructions so the active game retrieves its own guidance. Keep archived material available for its own saved campaigns. This removes a possible source of confusion; it is not proof that it caused any particular reply. |

## What to preserve and how to check the changes

Preserve the four core powers, twelve class choices, six-option/two-pick development boards, independent rarity rolls, Luck, creative creature forms, real dice and permanent owner-specific developments. Existing instructions already require proactive opposition, useful distinctive allies, standing orders, a two-approach minor-obstacle ceiling and visible quest endings. Those need behavioral checks as well as clear wording.

Use authored test scenes for a bound creature and a contrasting mobile or distributed creature. Check a complete starting build, ordinary creative power use, a decisive victory, a mixed result that still closes its objective, a surprise during domain activity, and a scene where an acquired upgrade changes the available plan. Check that Origin, Title, gear and ally capabilities affect relevant choices too. These are proposed development-time checks; this audit adds no per-turn model evaluation or extra gameplay tool loop.

## Accepted decisions and corrections

The player approved the changes with these corrections: retain the Age of Convergence lore and world introduction before Class selection; restore numbered levels and fourth-level rewards; remove any 70/30 adventure/strategy recommendation and let the GM choose useful scene scale. Luck remains internal, starts at 9 and grows automatically by 1 per earned level up to 20, with no allocation, visible statistic or level-up line. Regular levels grant two Core and two Sovereign upgrades; each fourth level adds two major refinements. All relevant boards remain six options/two picks, with twelve Classes/one pick. Origin and Title rewards remain additional. See the current release document for implementation and validation.
